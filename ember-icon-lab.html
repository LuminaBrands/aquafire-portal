Ember mark — three options

Each is vector or CSS, so one element scales from the 22 px tip bubble to the 60 px launcher and re-colours with the theme. Sizes below are the ones the mark actually renders at today. Motion is live — watch for ten seconds; none of the loops line up.

Current — Orb

A static radial-gradient sphere. Reads as a bead or a planet more than fire, and holds completely still next to a page that is otherwise alive.

Option 1 — Wick

A real flame silhouette, asymmetric so it never looks like a logo template. Two motion cycles of different lengths (4.3 s shape, 2.9 s core) plus two gradient cycles (5.1 s, 6.7 s) mean the loop does not visibly repeat. Most literally “fire”; also the most detail to lose at 22 px.

Option 2 — Coal

No flame outline: a living ember. The silhouette itself morphs on eight border-radius values, and a hot spot wanders inside it. Survives small sizes best of the three, because there is no fine detail to lose — at 22 px it is still an irregular glowing shape. Reads premium and abstract rather than literal.

Option 3 — Vapor

The only one that says what the product is: a flame whose tip stops being flame and becomes vapor. Two plumes rise, spread and fade on staggered loops. Conceptually the strongest fit for a water-vapor fireplace; the plumes are the first thing to disappear at small sizes, so it needs the base to carry it alone at 22 px.

In situ — where it has to work

Tip bubble (22 px), chat mini-avatar (26 px), widget header (40 px), corner launcher (60 px). The launcher is the one case where the mark sits on its own plate rather than on the page.
