Installer Field Notes

What our senior install crews do on site, past the published dimensions. Almost all of it is about controlling air — around the enclosure, and through the unit.

Framing

Be slightly generous on width

The widths on this page are nominal + ⅜" — ⅛" over the + ¼" minimum printed in the spec sheets. That extra room lets the unit seat without being forced, keeps air moving around the internals, and gives light strips, fans and sensors a buffer so nothing gets compressed while the insert is worked into place.

Depth and height are unchanged, and the margin stops here. The insert hangs from ½" flanges, so cutting wider than this starts eating into what the unit rests on.

Working to someone else's drawing? Check whether its clearance is called out overall or per side before you cut — both conventions turn up on shop drawings.

Sealing

The ideal: frame it, drywall it, seal it

The best outcome is agreed with the builder before anything is cut: the opening framed to spec, and the entire bottom enclosure drywalled and sealed. Open wall cavities behind or beneath the fireplace let air move through the enclosure, and that air pulls the flame down and drives vapor onto components.

Sealing

Tape is the fallback — and it goes on now

If you arrive to open framing, or you can feel air moving around the enclosure, seal the unit that day. Flip it to expose the underside and wrap the front and back edges with 3"-wide black high-quality tape (Gorilla tape or equivalent) — the width matters, it has to cover the edge properly.

Do it while the enclosure is open. Sealing after symptoms appear means pulling an installed unit back out.

Sealing

Seal the DP kit to the structure

Where a unit calls for a downdraft prevention kit, the kit only works if it is sealed against the surrounding structure. In high-rise condos, where the wall assemblies breathe, an unsealed DP kit has been the cause of major installation failures.

Airflow

Seal the cavity, not the vents

Sealing is about the wall cavity — the unit still has to breathe. Aquafire inserts need at least 50 square inches of air intake per 20" of unit length, through the top lid or, if the lid is covered by a decorative top or media, through a toe kick beneath the enclosure.

Before you leave, check nothing heavy is sitting on top of the unit and walk the customer through keeping it clear. This matters more, not less, once the enclosure is fully sealed.

HVAC

Why this keeps coming up

A lot of current construction runs “open plenum” positive-pressure HVAC. That trend routinely puts air pressure into fireplace enclosures, which is why sealing is now a default step on new builds rather than a fix for a problem house. Keep supply vents from blowing directly at the insert wherever the layout allows.

Handoff

Say it before you build it

Frame sealing to the builder and the customer as proactive best practice, not a repair. It heads off the service calls that cost everyone time later — replacing light strips and fans on a unit that has to come back out of a finished wall — and it keeps the fireplace looking right for the life of the install.
